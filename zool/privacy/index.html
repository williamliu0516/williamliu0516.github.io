Zool Privacy Policy

Last updated: 17 October 2026

Zool is a video player for Apple devices. It plays films and series from storage and media servers that you set up yourself. This page explains what data the app keeps, where data goes, and how to remove it. Zool is made by Xiawei Liu (“we”).

In short

  • We run no server for the app and collect no personal data. There is no account to create.
  • The app has no advertising, no analytics and no third-party tracking code, and it does not track you across other companies’ apps or websites.
  • The app connects to the sources you add, to TMDB for posters and descriptions, to TheIntroDB and IntroDB for intro and credits times, to plex.tv if you sign in with Plex, to OpenSubtitles when you search for subtitles, and to Apple.
  • The addresses and passwords of your sources are kept in your Keychain. Nothing about your library or what you watch is sent to us.

Data that leaves your device

Your sources

The app connects only to the sources you add, and to the addresses those servers point it to.

  • WebDAV: your user name and password go with each request (HTTP Basic authentication).
  • SMB: your user name and password are used to sign in to the share.
  • Jellyfin and Emby: your user name and password are sent once to sign in. The access token the server returns is kept in memory only.
  • Plex: you sign in on Plex’s own website in your browser, so the app never sees your Plex password. Plex gives the app an access token and the list of your servers.
  • SFTP: your user name and your password, or your private key, are used to sign in to the server over SSH. The first time, the app shows the server’s key fingerprint and trusts it only once you confirm.
  • FTP: your user name and password are sent to the server unencrypted, as FTP does; the app says so when you add an FTP server.
  • Folders on your device: files you choose in the Files app (on this device, in iCloud Drive or on a USB drive; on a Mac, any folder) are read in place. The app keeps a bookmark to the folder on this device only; nothing is sent anywhere.

Jellyfin, Emby and Plex servers are told the app’s name and version, the kind of device (iPhone, iPad, Mac or Apple Vision Pro), the platform and the random identifier described above. Your device’s name is never sent. While you watch, the app tells these servers which item is playing, the position, and whether it is playing, paused or stopped, about every ten seconds, so the server keeps your place. It also reads your resume points and watched marks from them. WebDAV and SMB servers receive no such reports.

Some servers hand playback to another address, such as the storage service behind them. The app then reads the video from that address, and your password is not sent there. Whoever runs your server, and any such service, handles this traffic under their own terms. Use an https:// address where you can: over http://, your password and your video travel unencrypted.

TMDB (posters and descriptions)

To find posters and descriptions, the app sends requests to The Movie Database (TMDB). They contain:

  • the title and year read from a folder or file name, or an ID from an NFO file in your folders or from your media server;
  • when you match a title by hand, the words and year you type into that search;
  • the language for the results, the same as the app’s language;
  • an API key: the app’s own, or yours if you entered one.

Images are then loaded from TMDB’s image server. Like any internet request, these show TMDB your IP address. They do not include your server’s address, your user names or passwords. TMDB handles them under its privacy policy.

This application uses TMDB and the TMDB APIs but is not endorsed, certified, or otherwise approved by TMDB.

TheIntroDB and IntroDB (intro and credits times)

While a film or episode plays, the app asks TheIntroDB (theintrodb.org) and IntroDB (introdb.app) where its intro, recap and credits are, so it can offer Skip Intro. It sends the title’s TMDB or IMDb ID, the season and episode, and to TheIntroDB the video’s length (to tell one cut of a film from another). Like any internet request, these show them your IP address. They do not include your server’s address, file names, user names or passwords. They are asked only when your own server and the video’s chapters have no such times.

OpenSubtitles (subtitle search)

Only when you search for subtitles in the player: the app sends OpenSubtitles.com the title’s TMDB ID (for an episode, the series’ ID with the season and episode), a short fingerprint of the video file (the OpenSubtitles “movie hash”, worked out on your device from the file’s size and its first and last 64 KB; the video itself is not sent), the title when there is no ID, and the languages you chose, with the app’s API key. If you sign in, your user name and password are sent once, and the access token it returns is used for downloads. Like any internet request, these show OpenSubtitles your IP address. They do not include your server’s address or your source’s user names and passwords. OpenSubtitles handles them under its own privacy policy, published at opensubtitles.com.

Searching your Jellyfin, Emby or Plex server for subtitles asks only that server.

Plex (plex.tv)

Only if you add a Plex server: the app asks plex.tv for a sign-in code, opens Plex’s sign-in page in your browser, then receives your access token and your list of servers. These requests carry the app’s name and version, the platform, the kind of device and the random identifier. Plex handles them under its own privacy policy, published at plex.tv.

Apple

  • Installing and updating the app go through the App Store, under Apple’s privacy policy.
  • If you have turned on Share With App Developers (Settings > Privacy & Security > Analytics & Improvements; on a Mac, in System Settings), Apple shares crash reports and usage statistics for the app with us. Apple runs this, and you can turn it off there at any time.
  • iCloud Keychain, when you use it, is run and encrypted by Apple; we cannot read it. iCloud Sync stores its data in your own iCloud account, in this app’s private CloudKit database; we cannot read it.
  • SharePlay: when you watch together in a FaceTime call, the app sends the others in the call the title, the season and episode, the video’s file name and size (so their own copy can be found in their library), and play, pause and seek. This goes through Apple’s SharePlay, end-to-end encrypted, to the people in the call only; we receive nothing.
  • Handoff: the title on screen, or the video playing with its source, file path and position, is offered to your other devices signed in to the same Apple Account, through Apple.
  • Translated subtitles: translation runs on your device with Apple’s Translation; the languages are downloaded from Apple when you first use them.

Your local network

  • The app asks for local network access to reach servers on your network. While you add an SMB server, it also looks for SMB servers nearby (Bonjour).
  • While a video plays, the app runs a small web server on your device that hands the video to Apple’s player. It can be reached from your local network, so that an AirPlay receiver can play the video. Each playback uses a new address with a long random part.

Your home (Lights)

Only if you turn on Lights in Settings (iPhone, iPad, Apple Vision Pro): the app asks for access to your Home, lists its lights and scenes so you can choose, and while a film plays dims the ones you chose, then puts them back. It reads and changes only those. This stays between your device and your accessories; data from your Home is not sent to us and is not used for advertising or anything else.

Links

Links in the app, such as TMDB’s website, the open-source licenses and this website, open in your browser.

What the app does not do

  • It does not ask for your name, email address or phone number, and has no account.
  • It does not use your location, contacts, photos, camera or microphone. Audio analysis (finding intros across episodes, lining subtitles up with speech) listens to the film’s own sound as it plays, on the device.
  • It does not use data from your Home for anything but the lights you chose.
  • It does not show ads and does not use the advertising identifier.
  • It does not send us your library, what you watch, or your settings.

Diagnostics reports

In Settings, Diagnostics can make a report to help with a problem. It contains the app and system versions, the device model, the kind and host name of each source, counts from your library, recent scan results and errors, recent playback problems with the file names involved, recent messages from the playback engine, recent iCloud Sync steps and errors, if you use Lights how many homes, rooms, lights and scenes were found (not their names), and the latest MetricKit reports. Passwords, user names, tokens, keys, request header values and the parameters of web links are removed. The report is made on your device and leaves it only when you choose Share Report and send it somewhere yourself, for example by email to us.

When you contact us

If you email support@xiaweiliu.com, we receive your email address and whatever you send, including any report you attach, and use them only to answer you. Your emails stay in our mailbox until you ask us to delete them.

Children

The app is not directed at children, and it collects no personal data from anyone, children included.

Removing your data

  • Remove a source in the Files tab. This deletes its Keychain item, on every device that shares it through iCloud Keychain, together with its library files and its poster copies. Nothing on the server is deleted. Resume positions, watched marks and cached images stay until you delete the app (the image cache is emptied every six months).
  • Your TMDB key: clear it in Settings.
  • OpenSubtitles: sign out in Settings > Online Subtitles.
  • iCloud Sync: turn it off in Settings to stop syncing; what this device holds stays. To delete what the app keeps in iCloud, use your device’s iCloud storage settings (manage storage, choose Zool, delete its data). Deleting the app does not delete its iCloud data.
  • Lights: turn Lights off in Settings; to take away the app’s access to your Home, use the system’s Privacy & Security settings (Home).
  • Watch statistics: the record of plays is deleted with the app.
  • Delete the app to remove the rest of its data from that device. On a Mac, also delete the folder ~/Library/Containers/com.xiaweiliu.zool. Keychain items can outlast the app, so remove your sources in the app first; otherwise, on a Mac, delete the items named “Reel sources” and “Reel TMDB key” in Keychain Access.
  • Our side: we hold nothing about you unless you emailed us. To have your emails deleted, write to support@xiaweiliu.com.

Changes

This policy describes the app as of the date at the top. If a later version handles data differently, this page is revised and the date changes.

离开你设备的数据

你的片源

app 只连接你添加的片源，以及这些服务器让它去的地址。

  • WebDAV：每次请求都带上你的用户名和密码（HTTP Basic 认证）。
  • SMB：用你的用户名和密码登录共享。
  • Jellyfin 和 Emby：登录时发送一次用户名和密码，服务器返回的访问令牌只保存在内存里。
  • Plex：你在浏览器里登录 Plex 自己的网站，app 看不到你的 Plex 密码。Plex 把访问令牌和你的服务器列表交给 app。
  • SFTP：用你的用户名和密码，或者你的私钥，通过 SSH 登录服务器。第一次连接时 app 显示服务器密钥的指纹，你确认后才信任。
  • FTP：用户名和密码按 FTP 的方式不加密地发给服务器；添加 FTP 服务器时 app 会说明。
  • 设备上的文件夹：你在“文件”App 里选的文件夹（本机、iCloud 云盘、U 盘；Mac 上任何文件夹）直接读取。app 只在这台设备上记住这个文件夹，不发往任何地方。

Jellyfin、Emby 和 Plex 服务器会收到 app 的名称和版本、设备类型（iPhone、iPad、Mac 或 Apple Vision Pro）、平台，以及上面说的随机标识符， 不会收到你设备的名称。播放时，app 大约每十秒告诉这些服务器正在播放哪一项、播放到哪里、是在播放、暂停还是已停止，让服务器记住你的进度； app 也会从这些服务器读取续播位置和已看标记。WebDAV 和 SMB 服务器不会收到这类回报。

有些服务器会把播放转到另一个地址，比如它背后的存储服务。这时 app 从那个地址读取视频，不会把你的密码发过去。 这些流量由你的服务器以及这类服务的运营者按他们自己的条款处理。尽量使用 https:// 地址：用 http:// 时，你的密码和视频都以明文传输。

TMDB（海报和简介）

为了找到海报和简介，app 会向 The Movie Database（TMDB）发送请求，内容包括：

  • 从文件夹名或文件名里读出的片名和年份，或者来自你文件夹里 NFO 文件、或你的媒体服务器的条目编号；
  • 你手动匹配作品时，在那个搜索框里输入的文字和年份；
  • 结果的语言，和 app 的界面语言一致；
  • 一个 API 密钥：app 自带的，或者你填写的。

之后 app 从 TMDB 的图片服务器加载图片。和所有联网请求一样，TMDB 能看到你的 IP 地址。这些请求不包含你的服务器地址、用户名或密码。 TMDB 按它的隐私政策处理这些请求。

This application uses TMDB and the TMDB APIs but is not endorsed, certified, or otherwise approved by TMDB.（本应用使用 TMDB 和 TMDB API，但未经 TMDB 认可、认证或批准。）

TheIntroDB 和 IntroDB（片头片尾时间）

播放电影或剧集时，app 向 TheIntroDB（theintrodb.org）和 IntroDB（introdb.app）查询片头、前情提要和片尾在哪里，用来显示“跳过片头”。 发送的是这部片的 TMDB 或 IMDb 编号、季和集，向 TheIntroDB 还发送视频长度（用来区分同一部片的不同版本）。和所有联网请求一样，它们能看到你的 IP 地址。 这些请求里没有你的服务器地址、文件名、用户名或密码。只有你的服务器和视频的章节里都没有这些时间时才查询。

OpenSubtitles（搜索字幕）

只在你在播放器里搜索字幕时：app 向 OpenSubtitles.com 发送这部片的 TMDB 编号（剧集是剧的编号加季和集）、视频文件的一个短指纹 （OpenSubtitles 的“movie hash”，在你的设备上由文件大小和开头、结尾各 64 KB 算出；视频本身不发送）、没有编号时的片名、你选的语言， 以及 app 的 API key。如果你登录，用户名和密码只发送一次，之后用它返回的访问令牌下载。和所有联网请求一样，OpenSubtitles 能看到你的 IP 地址。 这些请求里没有你的服务器地址，也没有片源的用户名和密码。OpenSubtitles 按它在 opensubtitles.com 上公布的隐私政策处理。

在你的 Jellyfin、Emby 或 Plex 服务器上搜字幕，只问那台服务器。

Plex（plex.tv）

只在你添加 Plex 服务器时：app 向 plex.tv 申请一个登录码，在浏览器里打开 Plex 的登录页面，然后收到你的访问令牌和服务器列表。 这些请求带有 app 的名称和版本、平台、设备类型和随机标识符。Plex 按它在 plex.tv 上公布的隐私政策处理这些请求。

Apple

  • 安装和更新 app 经由 App Store，适用 Apple 的隐私政策。
  • 如果你打开了“与 App 开发者共享”（设置 > 隐私与安全性 > 分析与改进；Mac 上在“系统设置”里），Apple 会把这款 app 的崩溃报告和使用统计提供给我们。 这由 Apple 管理，你可以随时在那里关闭。
  • 你使用 iCloud 钥匙串时，它由 Apple 运行和加密，我们无法读取。iCloud 同步的数据存在你自己的 iCloud 账户里这个 app 的私有 CloudKit 数据库，我们无法读取。
  • 同播共享：在 FaceTime 通话里一起看时，app 把片名、季和集、视频的文件名和大小（让对方在自己的片库里找到同一个文件）以及播放、暂停、跳转发给通话里的其他人。 经 Apple 的同播共享传递，端到端加密，只到通话里的人；我们收不到任何东西。
  • 接力：屏幕上的作品，或者正在播的视频（片源、文件路径、位置），通过 Apple 提供给登录同一个 Apple 账户的你的其他设备。
  • 翻译字幕：翻译在你的设备上用 Apple 的翻译完成；第一次用某种语言时从 Apple 下载语言包。

你的本地网络

  • app 申请本地网络权限，用来连接你网络上的服务器；添加 SMB 服务器时，还会在附近查找 SMB 服务器（Bonjour）。
  • 播放视频时，app 会在你的设备上运行一个小型网页服务，把视频交给 Apple 的播放器。本地网络上的设备可以访问它，这样 AirPlay 接收端才能播放； 每次播放都使用一个新的、带有很长随机部分的地址。

你的家（灯光）

只有在“设置”里打开“灯光”时（iPhone、iPad、Apple Vision Pro）：app 请求访问你的“家庭”，列出灯和场景让你选，播放时把你选的调暗，结束后恢复。 只读取和改动这些。这些只在你的设备和配件之间；“家庭”的数据不发给我们，也不用于广告或其他用途。

链接

app 里的链接，比如 TMDB 网站、开源许可和本网站，会在你的浏览器里打开。

app 不做的事

  • 不要求你提供姓名、邮箱或电话号码，没有账号。
  • 不使用你的位置、通讯录、照片、相机或麦克风。音频分析（跨集找片头、让字幕对上对白）分析的是正在播放的片子自己的声音，在设备上完成。
  • 除了你选的灯，不把“家庭”的数据用在别处。
  • 不显示广告，不使用广告标识符。
  • 不把你的片库、观看内容或设置发送给我们。

诊断报告

“设置”里的“诊断”可以生成一份报告，帮助排查问题。报告包含 app 和系统版本、设备型号、每个片源的类型和主机名、片库的数量统计、最近的扫描结果和错误、 最近的播放问题及相关的文件名、播放引擎最近的消息、最近的 iCloud 同步步骤和错误、使用“灯光”时找到了几个家、房间、灯和场景（不含名称），以及最新的 MetricKit 报告。密码、用户名、令牌、密钥、请求头的值和网页链接里的参数都会被去掉。 报告在你的设备上生成，只有你选择“分享报告”并自己发出去（比如用邮件发给我们）时才会离开设备。

删除你的数据

  • 移除片源：在“文件”标签里移除。这会删除它的钥匙串项目（包括通过 iCloud 钥匙串同步到其他设备上的），以及它的片库文件和海报副本， 服务器上的文件不会被删除。播放进度、已看标记和缓存的图片会保留到你删除 app 为止（图片缓存每六个月清空一次）。
  • 你的 TMDB 密钥：在“设置”里清空。
  • OpenSubtitles：在“设置”的“在线字幕”里退出登录。
  • iCloud 同步：在“设置”里关掉就不再同步，这台设备上的留着。要删掉 app 存在 iCloud 里的数据，在设备的 iCloud 储存空间设置里（管理储存空间，选 Zool，删除数据）。删除 app 不会删除它在 iCloud 里的数据。
  • 灯光：在“设置”里关掉“灯光”；要收回访问“家庭”的权限，到系统的“隐私与安全性”设置（家庭）。
  • 观看统计：播放记录随 app 删除。
  • 删除 app：删除这台设备上其余的数据。Mac 上还要删除文件夹 ~/Library/Containers/com.xiaweiliu.zool。 钥匙串项目可能在删除 app 后仍然保留，所以请先在 app 里移除片源；否则在 Mac 的“钥匙串访问”里删除名为“Reel sources”和“Reel TMDB key”的项目。
  • 我们这边：除了你发来的邮件，我们没有任何关于你的数据。如需删除你的邮件，请写信到 support@xiaweiliu.com。
